What POI Business Interiors actually does (public model)

POI Business Interiors is a Toronto-area workplace interiors firm. On its own website it describes itself as "a leading supplier of office, healthcare and educational product solutions" and lists a comprehensive array of services including audio-visual technology integration, facility management, relocation and moves, planning and design, and leasing through Steelcase Financial Services. Its homepage states that "POI creates workspaces featuring business furnishings, office furniture & interior solutions for corporate, education & healthcare," and it is publicly identified as a Steelcase Premier Partner, anchoring its product offer around the Steelcase line. POI operates from the Toronto and North York area with additional Ontario locations, and serves corporate, healthcare, education, and government clients. POI Business Interiors LP is also listed as a supplier partner under OECM's Furniture, Mattresses and Related Services agreement.

Read plainly, that is a full-service contract interiors model: a firm that can take a large organization through furniture, audiovisual technology, facility management, planning, and the move itself, built around a premium manufacturer relationship. For a major corporate or institutional project, that breadth is genuinely useful. The question for you is whether your project is that project.

Why buyers look for a POI alternative

People search for an alternative to a large interiors firm for a few practical reasons, and none of them are a knock on the incumbent. The most common is fit. A full-building services model is organized around sizeable, multi-trade projects; if you are buying furniture rather than reconstructing a floor, you may be looking for a supplier whose core business is exactly the part you need. A second reason is product transparency: buyers increasingly want to see real products, specifications, and pricing before they call, rather than starting every enquiry with a consultation. A third is breadth of brand. A firm anchored to one premium manufacturer line gives you depth in that line; a multi-line dealer can mix manufacturers to hit a budget and a spec at the same time. A fourth is simply scale of relationship: smaller organizations sometimes want to be a meaningful customer rather than a small account inside an enterprise pipeline.

If any of those describe you, the right alternative is not a smaller version of the same model. It is a different model: a dealer whose whole business is commercial furniture, design, delivery, and service.
