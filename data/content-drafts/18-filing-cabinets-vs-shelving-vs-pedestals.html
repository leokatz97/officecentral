When to use each

Filing cabinets are for documents you keep but do not reach for constantly. Choose vertical files when floor width is tight and you need depth, and lateral files when you want wider access, more capacity, and a usable top; a commercial lateral like the Global Premium Series lateral file is a typical example, available in two to five drawers. Open shelving and bookcases are for what people reach for often: binders, manuals, reference, and supplies, where scanning a shelf beats opening a drawer; a commercial unit like the Global bookcase comes in many sizes to fit the wall you have. Pedestals are personal storage at the desk, a box-box-file set of drawers, often mobile so it tucks under the worksurface; a mobile pedestal such as the Global Newland keeps each person's daily items and a few files within reach.

The questions that decide your mix

  • How much is active vs archive? Active, frequently-used items want open shelving or pedestals; archive documents want filing cabinets.
  • How much floor vs wall do you have? Tight floors favour vertical files and tall shelving; open walls suit lateral files that double as credenzas.
  • Is anything confidential? Sensitive records need lockable, and sometimes fire-rated, filing rather than open shelving.
  • Personal or shared? Pedestals for individual storage; lateral files and shelving for team and department storage.

Most offices end up with a mix: pedestals at the desks, lateral files and shelving in shared zones, and vertical or fire-rated files where records demand it. As more records go digital, many offices are shifting the balance toward open shelving and pedestals and away from banks of filing cabinets, but paper-heavy and regulated roles still need real filing.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I use filing cabinets or shelving for office storage? Use filing cabinets for documents you keep but do not reach for daily, especially anything confidential, and use open shelving for items people reach for often, like binders, manuals, and supplies. Most offices need both: filing for records and archive, shelving for active reference. Match the type to how often the contents are accessed.

What is the difference between a vertical and lateral file cabinet? A vertical file is narrow and deep, storing files front to back in a small floor footprint, which suits tight spaces and high document volume. A lateral file is wider and lower, storing files side to side with easier access and a usable top that can double as a credenza. Lateral files generally offer more capacity and flexibility; vertical files save floor width.

What is an office pedestal? A pedestal is a compact set of drawers for personal storage at a workstation, typically a combination of box drawers for supplies and a file drawer, and often mobile so it rolls under or beside the desk. It gives each person day-to-day storage and a few files within reach, separate from the shared filing and shelving used by the team.

How do I store confidential documents securely? Use lockable filing cabinets rather than open shelving, and for sensitive or irreplaceable records consider fire-rated filing. Keep confidential storage out of open, high-traffic areas, and assign access deliberately. Open shelving is fine for non-sensitive reference material but is the wrong place for anything that needs to be secured.

Do offices still need filing cabinets if records are digital? Many offices need fewer than they used to, and the balance is shifting toward open shelving and pedestals as paper volume drops. But paper-heavy and regulated roles, and anyone keeping signed, legal, or confidential originals, still need real, lockable filing. The right answer is usually less filing than before, not none.
