## FULL DRAFT (body HTML-ready)

**"Is this actually made in Canada?" is getting harder to answer — and more buyers are asking.** Search for Canadian-made office chairs and you'll find directories, home-office shops, and a lot of "proudly Canadian" banners that don't say where anything is actually built. Meanwhile the question is coming up more often: businesses and institutions want supply chains they can count on, procurement teams have buy-Canadian preferences, and after a couple of years of tariff and shipping uncertainty, "made here" has gone from a nice-to-have to a real factor in the decision.

This guide gives the straight answer for an Ontario business, school, clinic, or public-sector office: why Canadian-made matters for a *business* (not just for sentiment), the honest difference between made-in-Canada, designed-in-Canada, and assembled-in-Canada, the Canadian manufacturers worth knowing, and how to actually buy Canadian-made commercial furniture without taking a banner's word for it.

### Why Canadian-made matters for a business or institution

For a home buyer, buying Canadian is mostly values. For a business or institution, there are concrete reasons:

- **Procurement preference.** Many public-sector and institutional buyers have a stated preference — or in some cases a requirement — to favour Canadian suppliers and products. Canadian-made furniture clears that bar cleanly.
- **Supply-chain resilience + lead times.** Domestically manufactured furniture is less exposed to international shipping disruption and border costs — often shorter, more predictable lead times. ⚠️ keep specific lead-time claims non-committal per voice rules.
- **Parts + warranty service.** When the maker is Canadian, parts, replacements, and warranty claims are handled domestically — not through an offshore desk.
- **Quality match.** Canada's commercial furniture industry builds genuinely contract-grade product — this isn't "settle for local," it's some of the best in the category.

### Common Questions

**Why buy Canadian-made office furniture for a business?** Procurement preferences (especially in the public sector), more resilient supply chains and lead times, domestic parts and warranty service, and genuinely contract-grade quality. For a business it's a practical decision, not just a values one.

**What's the difference between "made in Canada" and "designed in Canada"?** "Made in Canada" means manufactured here and meets a legal Canadian-content threshold; "designed in Canada" means engineered by a Canadian company but possibly built elsewhere; "assembled in Canada" means parts from elsewhere put together here. "Canadian company" only tells you the head office is here. Always ask which one applies.

**Which office furniture brands are Canadian?** Several leading commercial makers are Canadian companies — including Global Furniture Group, ergoCentric, Keilhauer, and Teknion — many manufacturing in Ontario. ⚠️ Confirm specific lines with the dealer, as manufacturing locations vary by product.

**Can you furnish a whole office with Canadian-made furniture?** Largely, yes — Canadian manufacturers cover chairs, desks, workstations, soft seating, and storage at commercial grade. You can outfit most of an office domestically without compromising on quality.

**Is Canadian-made office furniture more expensive?** Not necessarily — commercial-grade Canadian product competes on price with imported contract furniture, and often wins on lead time and warranty service. The premium, where there is one, usually buys resilience and support.

**Does buying Canadian-made help with public-sector procurement?** It can — many broader-public-sector buyers have buy-Canadian preferences, and Canadian-made product satisfies them. Through an OECM Supplier Partner, Ontario broader-public-sector buyers can also procure without a separate tender.
