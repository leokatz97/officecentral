Where the broad-range route usually carries a project

One manufacturer for the whole floor

Most fit-outs are not a single hero chair; they are dozens of seats across very different rooms. A broad-range manufacturer lets you specify the all-day task fleet, the boardroom chairs, the guest seating, and the lounge from one catalogue, with finishes and a service relationship that line up. That coherence is hard to assemble from a handful of specialist lines, and it pays off when something needs to be added or matched two years later.

Price points that flex with the budget

A deep range means you are not forced to choose between a premium statement chair and nothing. You can put a design-forward specialist piece where it earns its keep and a well-built, value-priced contract chair where the job is simply all-day support, and keep the whole order inside one budget. That flexibility, multiplied across a floor, is often what makes a project work financially.

Comfort that survives a multi-user fleet

A chair in a private office serves one body; a chair in a shared task fleet serves dozens. For a floor where chairs are pooled across hot-desks and touchdown stations, that forgiving adjustability is often more valuable than a signature silhouette, and it is where a deep task range earns its keep.

How to judge any contract chair, whichever line

Specialist or broad-range, a contract chair earns its place by how it performs for the people who sit in it all day. Before you commit a fleet, run any shortlist through these checks:

  • Adjustability that fits real bodies. Seat height and depth, lumbar height or firmness, and arms that move, so one chair suits a range of users rather than an average one.
  • A tilt people will actually use. A recline that adjusts for tension and holds at a chosen angle encourages movement through the day, which is most of the comfort benefit.
  • Materials matched to the room. Mesh for breathability in a task fleet, upholstery where finish and warmth matter, and a fabric grade rated for the traffic it will see.
  • A contract-grade frame and warranty. The chair should be built and warranted for all-day, multi-user commercial use, with the terms on a verified specification, not implied.
  • Service behind the chair. Who plans the order, who delivers and assembles it, and who you call when a part needs replacing in year three.

Walk a shortlist through those five points and the specialist-versus-broad-range question usually answers itself, room by room. A design-led piece and a broad-range workhorse will both pass; the difference is which job each is doing in your space.

When a design-led specialist is the right choice

To keep this fair, here is the case for leading with a specialist line, plainly:

  • The chair is part of the brand. In a reception or client-facing space, a distinctive, well-crafted piece does real work that a generic chair cannot.
  • A designer has specified it. If the look, silhouette, or material has been chosen to fit the architecture, matching it matters.
  • Fewer, finer pieces suit the space. A showcase room is often better served by a small number of considered chairs than by catalogue depth.

None of that argues against breadth elsewhere. The strongest projects usually pair a specialist line where design leads with a broad range across the working floor, and a dealer who carries both can build exactly that mix.
