The reception desk: size, height, and access

The desk is the anchor. Size it so the receptionist has a real working area on the seated side and a counter on the visitor side, and place it where it greets people the moment they enter without blocking the entrance or sightlines. Plan two heights: a standing-height transaction counter for visitors and a seated work surface behind it. Include an accessible-height section of counter so the desk works for visitors using a wheelchair, which in Ontario supports AODA compliance. An L-shaped reception desk such as our L-shape reception unit gives you the counter, the work surface, and the footprint in one piece.

Waiting seating: comfort that survives traffic

Reception seating gets sat in by dozens of different people a week, so it has to be comfortable and durable, not just attractive. Choose commercial-grade frames rated for the traffic, and wipe-clean upholstery, vinyl or performance fabric, that holds up to constant use and cleaning, which matters most in healthcare and high-traffic public spaces. Guest chairs suit professional offices and keep people in their own space; benches and sofas suit higher-volume or more casual lobbies. A commercial guest chair like the Global Sidero, offered in many colours, is a typical reception choice that lets you match the brand. Many offices mix a few guest chairs with a bench for flexibility.

Layout and first impression

Position the desk to greet visitors immediately and keep clear circulation so the space never feels congested. Leave room for the waiting seating to sit comfortably without crowding the entrance, and keep pathways clear for accessibility. The finishes, signage, lighting, and a plant or two, carry the brand, but they work only on top of a functional layout. Because the reception area is small and high-stakes, it is worth planning before you buy; a design layout sorts the desk placement, seating, and circulation in one pass.

Frequently Asked Questions

What furniture do you need in an office reception area? At a minimum: a reception desk to greet and direct visitors, comfortable and durable waiting seating, occasional tables, and discreet storage for coats and materials, finished with branding, lighting, and signage. The desk and the seating are the core; everything else supports them. Choose commercial-grade throughout so the area survives constant traffic.

How big should a reception desk be? Size it to give the receptionist a real working surface on the seated side and a usable counter on the visitor side, scaled to the space so it greets people without blocking the entrance. Plan a standing-height transaction counter plus a seated work height, and include an accessible-height section. An L-shaped desk often fits these needs in a compact footprint.

What kind of seating is best for a waiting area? Commercial-grade seating with wipe-clean upholstery that handles constant use, comfortable enough that guests are at ease but durable enough for the traffic. Guest chairs suit professional offices and give each person their own space; benches and sofas suit busier or more casual lobbies. Many reception areas mix chairs and a bench. Avoid home-grade seating, which wears out fast in a lobby.

Does a reception desk need to be accessible? Yes, public-facing reception in Ontario should include an accessible-height counter section so visitors using a wheelchair can be served comfortably, which supports AODA compliance, along with clear circulation around the area. Build accessibility in at the layout stage rather than retrofitting it later.

How do you make a good first impression with a reception area? Combine a functional, well-placed desk with comfortable, clean, commercial-grade seating, clear circulation, good lighting, and finishes and signage that reflect the brand. The first impression comes from the space working smoothly as much as from how it looks, so get the layout and the durability right, then layer the brand on top.
