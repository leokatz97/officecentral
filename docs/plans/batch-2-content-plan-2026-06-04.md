## PART 1 — Competitor Teardown: theofficeshop.ca

**IP discipline (applies to every teardown below):** We extract **strategy, structure, and intent patterns only**. We never reproduce their copy, headings verbatim, or framing sentences. Pattern, not text. Their copy is their IP; the *idea* of "a manufacturer-by-manufacturer guide" is not.

### Cross-page pattern (what The Office Shop does on ALL 7 pages)

A single repeatable template, executed at scale:
- **Long-form** (1,400–3,500 words), keyword in H1 + repeated through H2s (sometimes to the point of stuffing — e.g. "ergonomic office furniture Toronto" 25+ times).
- **Heavy geo-clustering** — 14–24 city pages cross-linked in the footer of every resource; deep internal siloing by city × service × brand × industry.
- **Consultative/authority tone** — frames furniture as a holistic solution (consult → design → install → support); soft CTAs woven throughout (8+ per page), phone number repeated, "Schedule a consultation."
- **Partner-brand name-drops** (Global, Lacasse, Krug, Keilhauer, Humanscale, National, Three H, Workspace48) — but **no SKUs, no prices, no specs, no product links** on any page audited.
- **Inconsistent schema** — FAQ schema on some, none on others; missing LocalBusiness/Product/Breadcrumb almost everywhere.
- **25–30+ years in business** trust signal; no embedded testimonials/case-study data/research citations.

**The universal weakness = our universal wedge:** thin product data, generic manufacturer descriptions, no real comparison tables, no verified specs, no research-cited claims, no real numbers. Every page is built to push a phone call, not to *answer* the query.

## PART 2 — "Best Alternative to [Competitor]" Series (System C)

**This is the highest-risk content in the entire BBI program. Legal guardrails are built in from the first draft, not bolted on.**

### Legal guardrails (mandatory, inline in every brief)

Carry forward and *escalate* the Batch-1 competitor-reference audit discipline ([docs/reviews/track-a-claims-clearance-2026-06-04.md]). For every page naming a competitor:

1. **Every statement about a named competitor must be factual, verifiable, and based on public information** (their own website, public showroom/store model, publicly stated business model). Cite the public source in the brief.
2. **Describe their model and market position; position BBI on SUBSTANTIATED strengths.** "Source operates a national network of in-stock retail showrooms" (factual, public) — not "Source is low quality" (unverifiable, disparaging).
3. **NO disparagement. NO unverifiable negative claims. NO rigged rankings.** No "cheaper," "better quality," "they don't care about service" unless publicly substantiated and even then framed neutrally.
4. **The comparison table contrasts models, not insults** — e.g. "in-stock retail vs. multi-line dealer + design consult," "national big-box scale vs. Ontario-wide family service." Neutral descriptors only.
5. **BBI's differentiators must each be independently true and defensible:** multi-line dealer breadth, OECM Supplier Partner (verbatim: *"registered under our parent legal entity, Brant Basics, as an authorized OECM Supplier Partner"*), 60+ year family business, Ontario-wide delivery/install, in-house design consult. No claim that isn't already cleared.
6. **MANDATORY pre-publish gate:** legal/Steve sign-off on the named-competitor framing **before** the post goes even to draft-live. This is a hard gate in the build-state row — no exception.
7. **Trademark hygiene:** use competitor names nominatively (to refer to them) only; never imply endorsement, partnership, or affiliation; no competitor logos.

## PART 3 — Ontario Geo System (System B)

**This is a geo *system*, not name-swapping. The penalty risk here is real (doorway/thin-content). The rule below is non-negotiable.**

### Anti-thin-content rule (hard gate per page)

Every city page must carry **genuinely localized substance** or it does not ship:
- **Local business districts / corridors named and real** (e.g. Mississauga: Airport Corporate Centre, Meadowvale, Hurontario, City Centre; Ottawa: Kanata, ByWard/downtown core, federal precinct; London: Western/Fanshawe + downtown; Hamilton: HHS/healthcare + steel-district industrial).
- **Delivery/install reality for that city** — real lead times, real Ontario-wide logistics (confirmed Ontario-wide), real install coverage. This is the exact thing The Office Shop is *vague* about ("we serve 18 cities") — we get specific.
- **Local market context / data** — dominant sectors/buyers (Ottawa = federal + Ontario gov + BPS; KW = tech corridor; London = education + healthcare; Hamilton = healthcare + manufacturing), local procurement realities, OECM relevance where institutional.
- **Distinct intro, examples, and product picks** — different representative products/PDPs per city where the sector mix differs (institutional lateral files for Ottawa/gov; benching for KW tech; healthcare seating for Hamilton/London).
- **NEVER the same body with the city swapped.** A reviewer must be able to read any two city pages side by side and see materially different substance. If two pages would be 80%+ identical, **merge or cut**.

### What gets geo-multiplied (and what does NOT)

- ✅ **Commercial/local-intent only:** "office furniture [city]", "office furniture stores [city]", "[category] [city]", delivery/install [city].
- ❌ **NOT conceptual/informational guides** (office layout, ergonomics ROI, lease-vs-buy, design trends) — these are single canonical pages. Geo-multiplying a concept = textbook doorway content.

### What to improve (bake into the Batch-2 brief from draft v1)
